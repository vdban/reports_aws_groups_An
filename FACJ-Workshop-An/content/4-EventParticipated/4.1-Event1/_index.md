# Report: AWS Study Tour 2026 – Practical Preparation for a Career in Cloud & Data

## Purpose of the Event

AWS Study Tour 2026 was organized to provide students from Swinburne Vietnam (Ho Chi Minh City and Can Tho campuses) with an opportunity to visit Amazon Web Services (AWS) and gain firsthand insights into the real working environment of the cloud industry.

The event aimed to give participants a comprehensive understanding of the current job market in Cloud Computing and Data Engineering, highlight the gap between academic learning and industry expectations, and provide practical advice on improving competitiveness, building personal visibility, and overcoming the psychological barriers faced by students entering the technology industry.

---

## Speakers

- **Nguyen Tran Minh Duy** – Industry Liaison Officer, Swinburne Vietnam
- **Nguyen Gia Hung** – Head of Solution Architecture, Amazon Web Services Vietnam; Founder of First Cloud AI Journey
- **Banh Cam Vinh** – Data Engineer at Renova Cloud; AWS Community Builder
- **Nhu Tran** – Account Manager, Amazon Web Services Vietnam

---

## Key Highlights

### 1. Job Market and Cloud Industry Trends
**Speaker:** Nguyen Gia Hung

One of the most valuable sessions focused on the current Cloud job market and the skills required to remain competitive.

Key takeaways:

- The Cloud industry has become increasingly competitive, with even internship positions requiring knowledge of technologies such as Kubernetes, Cloud Native, and DevOps.
- Academic performance alone is no longer sufficient to secure employment.
- Approximately **90% of job opportunities**, and nearly **100% at AWS**, are obtained through networking and employee referrals rather than public job postings.
- Artificial Intelligence is reshaping recruitment. Companies are reducing entry-level hiring while expecting candidates to leverage AI effectively in their daily work.
- Students should position themselves around specific industries (Banking, Retail, Manufacturing, Healthcare, etc.) instead of focusing solely on job titles such as Data Engineer or DevOps Engineer.

---

### 2. The Gap Between University and Industry
**Speaker:** Banh Cam Vinh

This session explained the significant differences between academic projects and real-world software development.

Key points include:

- Academic datasets are usually clean and well-defined, whereas production data is often incomplete, inconsistent, and collected from multiple sources.
- Mistakes in university projects mainly affect grades, while mistakes in production systems can directly impact customers, business operations, and company revenue.
- Engineers should prioritize understanding system architecture and core concepts rather than relying solely on specific technologies or platforms.
- AI can assist with coding, documentation, and dashboard generation, but it cannot replace engineers in understanding business requirements, designing system architectures, or collaborating with stakeholders.

---

### 3. Overcoming Fear and Building Confidence
**Speaker:** Nhu Tran

The final session focused on personal development and overcoming psychological barriers.

Key messages included:

- People are not truly afraid of making mistakes—they are afraid of the consequences associated with those mistakes.
- Rather than trying to imitate others, students should develop their own strengths and unique identity.
- Open communication with family, mentors, and colleagues can reduce pressure and support long-term personal growth.
- Confidence and communication skills are essential for success in the technology industry.

## What I Learned

### Career Development Strategy

One of the most memorable concepts introduced during the event was the formula:

> **Success = Capability × Visibility × Consistency**

Where:

- **Capability** represents technical knowledge and professional skills.
- **Visibility** reflects how well you are recognized by recruiters and the professional community.
- **Consistency** represents continuous learning and long-term commitment.

If any of these three factors equals zero, the overall result becomes zero.

---

### Lifelong Learning

Continuous learning is essential in the rapidly evolving technology industry.

AWS certifications, technical skills, and personal projects should be updated regularly to remain competitive.

However, certifications mainly help candidates pass the initial screening process, while real-world projects demonstrate practical experience and problem-solving ability.

---

### Business-First Mindset

Technology exists to solve business problems.

A successful Cloud or Data Engineer should understand business workflows and requirements before designing technical solutions.

The true value of technology lies in delivering meaningful business outcomes rather than simply implementing advanced technical features.

Following the event, I plan to apply these lessons in several ways.

### Improving Personal Projects

- Use AI to review source code.
- Ask AI to evaluate projects from the perspective of a technical interviewer.
- Upgrade university projects with production-level practices such as:
  - CI/CD pipelines
  - Security improvements
  - Monitoring and logging
  - Scalability considerations

---

### Expanding Professional Networks

I plan to become more active in technical communities, including:

- First Cloud AI Journey
- AWS User Groups
- AWS Community Day

Networking will help me learn from experienced professionals and increase opportunities through referrals.

---

### Strengthening Communication Skills

The event emphasized that communication is not merely a soft skill but a critical professional skill.

Engineers should be able to:

- Discuss requirements with business stakeholders.
- Collaborate across different teams.
- Present technical solutions effectively.
- Explain and defend architectural decisions.

Participating in AWS Study Tour 2026 was an inspiring and valuable experience.

The speakers shared honest perspectives about today's technology job market, emphasizing both the challenges and opportunities available to students.

The event encouraged active interaction, allowing participants to ask questions directly about:

- Cloud Architecture
- Data Engineering
- Artificial Intelligence
- Career development
- Technical interviews

I also gained a deeper appreciation for the **Pay It Forward** mindset—learning from experienced professionals today and sharing knowledge with future generations tomorrow.

## Lessons Learned

The event left me with several important lessons:

- Artificial Intelligence is transforming the industry rather than eliminating opportunities.
- Engineers should develop an ownership mindset and take responsibility for their technical decisions.
- Systems should always be designed with scalability in mind.
- Continuous learning, practical projects, and professional networking are all essential for long-term career success.
- Persistence is one of the most important qualities. Success comes from continuous improvement rather than a single certification or project.
