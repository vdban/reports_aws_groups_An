# Event Report: AWS Study Tour 2026 — Practical Preparation for a Career in Cloud & Data

## Purpose of the Event

AWS Study Tour 2026 was organized to give Swinburne Vietnam students (Ho Chi Minh City and Can Tho campuses) an opportunity to visit and learn about the real working environment at Amazon Web Services (AWS). The program helps students gain a comprehensive view of the job market in Cloud Computing and Data Engineering, and highlights the gap between academic knowledge and enterprise requirements.

In addition, the event shared experiences to help students improve competitiveness, build personal brand (visibility), expand networking, and overcome psychological barriers when starting a career in technology.

---



## Speakers

- **Nguyen Tran Minh Duy** – Industry Liaison Officer, Swinburne Vietnam
- **Nguyen Gia Hung** – Head of Solution Architect, Amazon Web Services Vietnam; Founder of First Cloud AI Journey
- **Banh Cam Vinh** – Data Engineer at Renova Cloud; AWS Community Builder
- **Nhu Tran** – Account Manager, Amazon Web Services Vietnam

---



## Key Highlights



### 1. Job Market and Cloud Trends

**Speaker:** Nguyen Gia Hung

One of the most impressive topics was the real picture of today's Cloud job market.

Notable points:

- The market is highly competitive; even Intern positions require deep knowledge such as Kubernetes (K8s), Cloud Native, or DevOps.
- Degrees or high GPA no longer guarantee employment.
- About **90% of job opportunities** (and nearly **100% at AWS**) come from **networking and referral** rather than public job postings.
- AI is changing how companies hire. Businesses tend to reduce Junior hiring and prioritize candidates who can combine AI into their work.
- Students should position themselves by **business domain** (Banking, Retail, Manufacturing, Healthcare...) rather than focusing only on job titles like Data Engineer or DevOps Engineer.

---



### 2. The Gap Between School and Enterprise

**Speaker:** Banh Cam Vinh

This session helped attendees understand the distance between learning and real work environments.

Highlighted points:

- In school, data is usually clean and problem requirements are fairly clear.
- In enterprise, data comes from many sources, is often incomplete, and changes constantly.
- A mistake in homework only affects grades, but a mistake in Production can cause revenue loss or impact customers.
- What matters most is understanding **platform architecture** rather than learning only specific tools.
- AI can help write code or create dashboards, but cannot yet replace engineers in understanding business problems, designing systems, and communicating with departments.

---



### 3. Overcoming Fear to Grow

**Speaker:** Nhu Tran

The session focused on psychological barriers many students face when entering the job market.

Key messages:

- What we fear is not making mistakes, but the consequences of mistakes.
- Each person should build their own path rather than trying to become a copy of someone else.
- Proactively communicating with family, colleagues, and community helps reduce pressure and grow faster.
- Confidence and communication skills are very important in technology environments.

## What I Learned



### Career mindset and development strategy

After the Study Tour, I drew many important lessons about career direction.

#### Success formula

> **Success = Capability × Visibility × Consistency**

Where:

- **Capability** is professional competence.
- **Visibility** is how well the community and recruiters know you.
- **Consistency** is persistence and continuous development.

If any of the three factors equals **0**, the final result is also **0**.

---



### Lifelong Learning

Learning does not end after graduation.

AWS certifications, personal projects, and regularly updating skills increase competitiveness in the job market.

However, certifications only act as an **initial filter**; real projects create the difference.

---



### Business-first mindset

A good engineer not only knows technology but must also understand business problems.

The ultimate goal of Data Engineering or Cloud Engineering is not building complex systems, but creating business value through data.

## Applying to Work and Study

After the event, I plan to apply what I learned in these ways:

### Upgrade personal projects

- Use AI to review source code.
- Have AI play the role of recruiter to evaluate projects.
- Add Production elements such as:
  - CI/CD
  - Security
  - Monitoring
  - Logging
  - Scalability

---



### Expand Networking

Actively join communities such as:

- First Cloud AI Journey
- AWS User Group
- AWS Community Day

This expands relationships and increases referral opportunities.

---



### Develop communication skills

The session helped me realize Communication is not just a soft skill but a required skill at work.

An engineer needs to:

- Exchange requirements with Business.
- Work with multiple departments.
- Present solutions.
- Defend technical decisions.

## Event Experience

Participating in AWS Study Tour was a very memorable experience.

What impressed me most was speakers sharing very realistic views about today's job market — neither overly optimistic nor creating excessive pressure.

The program atmosphere was very open, allowing students to ask direct questions about:

- Cloud Architecture
- Data Engineering
- AI
- Career direction
- Interview skills

Through this I also understood the **Pay It Forward** spirit — after learning from the community, each person should share knowledge back with the next generation.

---



## Lessons Learned

After the program, I drew several important lessons:

- AI does not reduce job opportunities, but increases requirements for young engineers.
- Need to prepare an **Ownership** mindset, taking responsibility for technical decisions.
- Always design systems with future scalability in mind (**Design for Scale**).
- Keep learning, build real projects, and expand networking.
- Persist in self-development because success comes from a long process, not from a few certifications or a single project.
